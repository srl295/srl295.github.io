Okay. A couple of things need explaining.
One, yes, this is the first post in three years.  But there are other posts on the [codehivetx.us](https://codehivetx.us) site.

But secondly, yes, this is a post about braille.

<!-- more -->

Almost before I was interested in languages, I was interested in accessibility.
In grade school, I helped out with OT/PT kids.  Classmates, just wheelchair bound. And nonverbal.
I also will always remember when someone came in who was blind. She talked about her experiences, and showed her Perkins embosser and other assistive technology.

I would doodle plans for the Micromouse competition, static RAM arrays as big as I could sketch (about five bits), and schemes for refreshable braille displays. I had heard of them, but they were huge, expensive, and especially expensive.

Well, they are still expensive, but they are no longer huge, thanks to piezo technology.

A few years ago, I bought one that was available in "not working" condition. It was very interesting inside, but I wasn't able to get it to work, even after discharging the scary 450V capacitor and then soldering a new USB connection to it.

Fast forward to 2026. A conversation about it, followed by meeting a community member who is recently blind and also trying to learn Braille (as I am), and it was time to start this project up again.
